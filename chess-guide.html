Chess vocabulary

What do the names in the move tracker mean?

Opening

A name for a recognizable sequence of early moves, such as the Italian Game. One ordinary move rarely tells you the entire opening.

Gambit

An opening that offers material, usually a pawn, for compensation such as time, development, or activity. The Queen’s Gambit is the classic example.

Special move

En passant, castling, and promotion are exceptional rules. They are not gambits and do not require sacrificing a piece.

Tactic

A pattern such as a fork, pin, skewer, or discovered attack. Not every position containing a tactic can be identified from move notation alone.

O-O / O-O-O

Castle toward the h-file (kingside) or toward the a-file (queenside). The king and rook move together.

×, +, #

Capture, check, and checkmate. Some classroom chess programs omit + and # from their move list but still enforce check.

=Q / =N

Pawn promotion to queen or knight. A pawn may also promote to a rook or bishop. Promotion is not restricted to queens.

Draw

Stalemate, insufficient material, repetition, and move-count rules can end a chess game without a winner. See the Draw Rules lesson.
